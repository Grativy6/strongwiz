# Laboratory policy interfaces

Strongwiz exposes three versioned, control-owned policy profiles. They are
supplied around the reasoning process; they are not generated or amended by a
model. Model output cannot create consent, standing, permission,
authorization, responsibility, or a release obligation.

These are bounded software profiles, not complete ethical systems, legal
advice, compliance certifications, or sources of jurisdiction. A passing
review says only that the supplied fields satisfied the implemented profile.

## PEA Core v1.1.3

The `PEAReview` interface is a quiet, non-self-executing review under an
external grant. It carries explicit states for consent, standing, privacy,
reversibility, remedy, contestability, refusal, and human responsibility. Open
concerns remain visible. The review has `authority: NONE`: it may expose a
problem or support a human decision, but it cannot decide, grant, execute, or
become sovereign.

## PECAN v1.0.4

The `ConsequentialCrossing` interface preserves four distinct stages:

`description -> recommendation -> permission -> authorization`

No later stage may omit the earlier supplied distinctions. Description is not
recommendation. Recommendation is not permission. Permission is not
authorization. Proof, success, confidence, repetition, or model fluency cannot
advance the ladder. Strongwiz reports that a crossing may proceed only when
authorization is explicitly marked as externally supplied.

## SEED v0.3

The `SEEDReleaseReview` interface checks the human-facing release boundary. A
candidate output names its chosen goal, claim status, uncertainty, authority
limits, privacy considerations, correction path, reopening condition, and
natural stop. Claim status uses a closed bounded taxonomy. Uncertainty,
authority, and privacy are each explicitly supplied or marked not applicable;
unresolved or refused burdens cannot clear release. The requested attention
must also fit the externally supplied task-grant budget. Release requires
preserved user agency and refuses manufactured dependency or model-personhood
claims.

SEED does not demand minimal word count. It asks the release to consume no more
agency, attention, time, imagination, or continuation than the work requires,
while preserving enough context to understand uncertainty and correct the
result.

## Control ownership

An integration should obtain these records from its external control plane,
bind them to the active task grant, and preserve them in receipts. Every
external-effect decision binds the exact task, goal, scope, observation,
proposal digest, action digest, grant, and output destination. The execution
coordinator rejects a route produced under a different control snapshot even
when every human-readable field appears similar.

Every task grant must also classify whether its effect requires human-facing
release review. That control-owned, digest-bound classification—not a model or
the submitted lab decision—determines whether SEED is required. A proposal that
itself declares an `OUTPUT` decision effect also requires SEED, even if the
grant classified its underlying domain action otherwise.

A PEA, PECAN, or SEED record binds the digest of that complete boundary context,
so the same review cannot be replayed for another grant, task, proposal, scope,
goal, output, or destination.

A model may identify a concern or draft a candidate review, but that output
remains a proposal until the external controller validates and supplies it.
Historical reasoning, receipts, later success, or a prior clear decision cannot
reactivate a revoked grant or manufacture authority through backflow.
